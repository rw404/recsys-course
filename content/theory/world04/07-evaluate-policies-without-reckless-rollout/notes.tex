\section{Evaluate policies without reckless rollout}

\begin{abstract}
Historical logs came from another policy, so offline policy value requires correction.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Concept}
A new policy cannot be judged by averaging rewards from actions selected by the old policy. Inverse propensity scoring reweights matching logged actions by the ratio between target and behavior probabilities. Doubly robust estimators combine that correction with a reward model. High-variance weights, poor overlap and unlogged propensities make estimates unreliable, so small guarded online experiments remain the final test.

\begin{equation*}
V̂_IPS(π) = (1/N) \sumₜ [ π(aₜ|xₜ) / μ(aₜ|xₜ) ] rₜ
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Key terms}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Off-policy evaluation.} Estimating one policy using data generated by another.
\item \textbf{Overlap.} Whether logged data includes actions the target policy might choose.
\item \textbf{Doubly robust.} An estimator combining propensity weighting with a reward model.
\end{itemize}
