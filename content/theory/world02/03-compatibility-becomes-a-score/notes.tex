\section{Compatibility becomes a score}

\begin{abstract}
A cheap geometric score lets one user vector compare against many item vectors.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Concept}
The two towers are trained so positive user–item pairs have a larger dot product or cosine similarity than unrelated pairs. Dot product also carries vector magnitude; cosine similarity compares direction only. Temperature controls how sharply the contrastive objective separates candidates. The score is useful for retrieval, but it is not a calibrated probability and does not include every business or slate-level consideration.

\begin{equation*}
s(u, i) = q_u^{\mathsf T}v_i      or      cos(q_u, v_i) = q_u^{\mathsf T}v_i / (‖q_u‖‖v_i‖)
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Key terms}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Dot product.} A fast compatibility score between two vectors.
\item \textbf{Cosine similarity.} Similarity based on vector direction after normalization.
\item \textbf{Temperature.} A scale that controls how sharp contrastive probabilities become.
\end{itemize}
